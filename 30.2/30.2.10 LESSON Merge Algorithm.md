## Understanding Merge Algorithms

Merging two lists is a common task in computer programming, often required when integrating data from multiple sources 
* e.g. combining sorted results from different files or merging live data feeds

-----

### Two Types of Merging

### 1\. Concatenating (Appending) aka smooshing togeather

  * This is the simplest form of merging.
  * You take all elements from one list and place them directly at the end of another list.
  * This method works regardless of whether the initial lists are sorted.

<!-- end list -->

```python
list1 = [1, 2, 3]
list2 = [4, 5, 6]
merged = list1 + list2
# merged = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]
```

### 2\. Interweaving (Merging Two Sorted Lists)

  * This more advanced method combines **two lists that are ALREADY sorted** into a single, combined sorted list.
  * The algorithm works by **comparing the smallest remaining items** (the items at the front of each list) and adding the smaller one to the new merged list.
  * This efficient process continues until both lists are completely moved into the final merged list.

#### Step-by-Step Algorithm Breakdown

The core process of interweaving (merging two sorted lists) involves iteratively taking the smallest available element from the *start* of the two input lists.

Let's merge:

  * List A: `[1, 4, 7]`
  * List B: `[2, 3, 6, 8]`
  * Result List: `[]`

| Step | List A (Remaining) | List B (Remaining) | Comparison | Action | Result List |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | `[1, 4, 7]` | `[2, 3, 6, 8]` | Compare 1 and 2 | Add 1 from A | `[1]` |
| **1** | `[4, 7]` | `[2, 3, 6, 8]` | Compare 4 and 2 | Add 2 from B | `[1, 2]` |
| **2** | `[4, 7]` | `[3, 6, 8]` | Compare 4 and 3 | Add 3 from B | `[1, 2, 3]` |
| **3** | `[4, 7]` | `[6, 8]` | Compare 4 and 6 | Add 4 from A | `[1, 2, 3, 4]` |
| **4** | `[7]` | `[6, 8]` | Compare 7 and 6 | Add 6 from B | `[1, 2, 3, 4, 6]` |
| **5** | `[7]` | `[8]` | Compare 7 and 8 | Add 7 from A | `[1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7]` |
| **6** | `[]` | `[8]` | A is empty | Add remaining 8 from B | `[1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8]` |

**Final Merged List: `[1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8]`**

> [!CAUTION]
>
> ### A Word of Warning: MergeSort vs. Merge
>
> Be careful **not to confuse** the process of merging two sorted lists with the complex **MergeSort algorithm**.
>
>   * **MergeSort** is a recursive sorting method that splits a single **unsorted** list, sorts the halves, and then merges them back together.
>   * The process you are studying is simply merging **already sorted lists**.
>   * **You are not implementing MergeSort** in this lesson. Focus only on the efficient, comparison-based interweaving logic.
